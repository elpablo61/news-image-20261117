# The World in One Day — Tuesday 17 November 2026

A newspaper front-page poster that sums up **Tuesday 17 November 2026** in a
single image (1024×1024 PNG). The date is the biggest thing on the page: a
red masthead band reading **TUESDAY 17 NOVEMBER 2026**, above five numbered
stories from that one day.

## The five stories

1. **Kerch Bridge hit again** — Ukraine struck the bridge to Crimea again; a
   road span collapsed and traffic was suspended, the second strike in under
   24 hours. Russia hit two bridges over the Dnipro in Kyiv and claimed
   1,336 Ukrainian drones intercepted.
2. **Fire in the Strait of Hormuz** — Two oil tankers struck naval mines and
   caught fire; a third US aircraft carrier, USS Theodore Roosevelt, arrived
   in the region.
3. **Xi, Trump and Putin to meet** — Trump arrived in Manila for the ASEAN
   summit and signed a trade statement with Thailand; China confirmed the
   first three-way Xi–Trump–Putin meeting, set for Thursday in Shenzhen.
4. **EU return hub in Rwanda** — Germany, Denmark, Austria, Greece and the
   Netherlands announced a joint migrant return hub in Rwanda for rejected
   asylum seekers.
5. **JPMorgan blames a bug** — A software bug (not a cyberattack) knocked out
   online banking and Zelle on Monday, the bank said.

Footer note: a ULA Vulcan rocket failed in flight, destroying 40 Amazon Leo
internet satellites.

Sources: Algenom, Level Headlines, OneTurn and FeedCast daily briefs,
17 November 2026.

## How it was made

All artwork was generated in-session with an image model (no text in the
generated art, no real people, no borrowed photos) and every word was
overlaid afterwards with a Python/PIL script using DejaVu fonts, so the text
is crisp and nothing is garbled. The three artworks: a collapsed sea bridge
at dawn (hero), two burning oil tankers in a strait, and the Shenzhen
skyline with flags.

## How to run

```sh
MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR=out python3 main.py
# writes out/entry.png (byte-identical to entry.png in this repo)
```

`main.py` uses only the Python standard library, needs no network, and
copies the bundled `entry.png` to `$MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR/entry.png`
(default `/output`). Running it twice produces identical bytes.
